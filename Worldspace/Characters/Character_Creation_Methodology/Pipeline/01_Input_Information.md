# Character Development Methodology — Stage 1: Input Information

**Pipeline position:** all the information that's required at the very beginning, before any processing
starts — everything that has to be true, known, or gathered about a character before Stage 2's diagnostic
toolkit can meaningfully be run on her. Built by tracing every single node, tool, and worked-example
dependency established across Stages 2, 3, 4, and 5 back to its raw input requirement — every field below is
justified by naming the specific downstream consumer that needs it, not asserted in the abstract.

**Two sections: Section A** (necessary — required to populate the nodes in Stages 2 through 5), and **Section
B** (optional — not required by any specific downstream node, but genuinely enriching to the material those
nodes produce).

**A note on the concrete examples throughout this file:** many entries below cite a real, specific instance
from the setting this methodology was originally built and battle-tested in, to make an abstract requirement
concrete. None of those specific examples (a war, a religion, a stat system, a named reference document) are
required — they're included as worked illustrations of *what kind of thing* a given field is asking for.
Substitute your own setting's equivalent freely.

---

# SECTION A — NECESSARY FIELDS

Every field in this section is required because a specific, named tool or node in Stage 2, 3, 4, or 5 cannot
be run, or cannot be run well, without it. This is the actual minimum viable input set for a full,
major-tier character-development pass.

---

## A1. Foundational Identity Facts

The bedrock facts that have to exist before any diagnostic work can even be framed.

- **Name.** Feeds the "Ten Ways We Come to Know a Character" delivery-channel audit (Stage 3) directly — a
  name is one of the ten named delivery channels, and this system's own source material flags a character's
  name as "one of the most consequential characterization decisions," capable of substituting for physical
  description entirely if chosen well, or denying an easy mental image if deliberately plain. Also required
  before the Characteristic Moment (Stage 4) can be written, since revealing her name is one of its checklist
  items.
- **Core nature or type, if the setting has more than one kind of being.** Governs which parts of any
  species/embodiment-specific lore even apply — in the setting this system was built in, that meant robot
  characters had a whole tier of specific reference material (a "Personality Module" tension, a Gen/Mark
  identity, an integrity/mortality threshold distinct from a human character's) that simply didn't apply to
  human characters at all. If your setting has an equivalent axis (species, embodiment, caste, magical
  nature), this single fact gates how much of that material is even relevant to a given character.
- **Any generational, cohort, or manufacturing-batch identity**, if the setting has one. Directly required for
  any "outlier within her own cohort" friction point to be usable at all — without knowing what group she
  belongs to by origin, there's nothing to check her against her peers for.
- **Age or apparent age.** Feeds the Background Taxonomy's Body sub-category (Stage 3) and is one of the "Ten
  Ways" delivery channels (demographic basics, part of the Characteristic Moment checklist).
- **Place of origin, at whatever granularity the setting supports** (city, region, world, faction territory).
  Feeds the Background Taxonomy's Environment sub-category (Stage 3) directly, and is required before any
  locality-belonging material can be applied. Also required before the Trigger-Type Design Pattern (Stage 5)
  can be built, since a character's trigger type has to be thematically derived from her own established
  nature — which usually includes where she's from.
- **Current residence and location, if different from origin.** A character who has relocated has a distinct
  Environment input from one who hasn't; also directly needed for the Normal World (Stage 4), which is
  specifically the *current* physical/social circumstances actively dramatizing the Lie, not necessarily her
  birthplace.
- **Physical appearance and reference art.** Feeds the Body sub-category of the Background Taxonomy (Stage 3),
  and is required for the Characteristic Moment's physical-characteristics checklist item (Stage 4). This
  system's own originating project treated reference art as the actual visual source of truth, not optional
  flavor — worth adopting as a default: if a character has a settled visual reference, let it govern rather
  than re-describing her from scratch.
- **Occupation, social role, or established function.** Feeds the "Ten Ways" delivery channel for Talents and
  Abilities, the Strength standalone field (Stage 3 — "what is she good at, apart from what hurts her"
  requires knowing what she does), and is frequently the anchor for her Want's General Goal (Stage 3).

---

## A2. Personality-Type Assignment

- **Type, and whatever finer-grained subtype/variant structure the typing system uses.** Explicitly mandatory,
  not optional — this has to exist *before* Stage 2 processing starts, since the core diagnostic principle
  this whole system runs on requires inner conflict to be *derived from* the type's own structural core-fear/
  core-desire tension, never invented independently of it. Stage 2's diagnostic tools are actually being run
  *against* this assignment, not used to produce it. This system was built around the Enneagram — see the
  sibling `../Enneagram/` folder for the full reference material and a "how to assign a type" tool — but any
  well-grounded typology with a comparable structure (a small number of core types, each with an internal
  fear/desire tension) could substitute.

---

## A3. Intended Story Role and Scope

Design-level facts about what kind of content this character is meant to carry, established before any
processing — distinct from the Stage 3 Front-Gate (Drift vs. Drive) and Stage 4 Front-Gate (MICE Quotient,
Character Hierarchy) *judgments*, which are diagnostic conclusions informed by these facts, not raw inputs
themselves.

- **Intended content tier** — is she meant to be a full principal character, a romanceable-but-not-recruitable
  fixture, a background named figure, a minor supporting character, or a walk-on? This is the single most
  load-bearing Stage 1 fact for calibrating how much of the rest of this methodology to actually run — it's
  the input the Stage 3 Front-Gate and Stage 4 Front-Gate trio are both making their determination from.
- **Base story vs. expansion/sequel placement, and whether she recurs across multiple installments.** Required
  before the Series-Spanning Arc Models (Stage 5) can be applied — the choice between "one overarching arc
  across the whole series" and "a fresh arc per installment" can't be made without first knowing whether she
  appears in more than one place at all.
- **Any already-known MICE Quotient leaning**, if the concept for her is already skewed toward one factor (a
  Milieu-heavy archive-narrator, an Event-driven crisis figure, a pure Character study) — not required to be
  fully resolved at Stage 1, since the MICE Quotient determination itself is Stage 4's job, but if this is
  already obvious from the initial concept, recording it here saves re-deriving it later.

## A4. Existing Biographical and Historical Material

The single most input-hungry category — this directly feeds the Ghost node (Stage 3), which needs "the past
wound that explains why she believes the Lie," and a wound needs an actual history to be drawn from.

- **Origin, construction, or birth history.** For a manufactured or constructed character, this includes any
  established creation chain (who or what was involved in her design, assembly, activation) and the
  circumstances of her coming-into-being. For an organically-born character, birthplace and early-life
  circumstances serve the same function.
- **Her position relative to the setting's own defining historical event or crisis, if it has one.** Did she
  exist before, during, or only after it? Was she directly caught up in it, or does she only know it as
  history? A setting's own major war, disaster, or turning point is frequently a primary source of Ghost
  material and needs to be established before Ghost construction starts, not discovered mid-process.
- **Any already-documented trauma, loss, or hardship.** Whatever's already on record — a specific documented
  incident, not a vague sense that "something bad probably happened." Directly feeds Ghost, and should be
  cross-checked against the Five Fundamental Types of Death (Social, Spiritual, Psychological, Professional,
  Physical) to identify which type, if any, is already in play before inventing a new one.
- **Existing relationships**, itemized by category, since each category feeds a different downstream need:
  - **Family or creator** — for a manufactured character, whoever built or activated her; for an organically-
    born one, her family of origin. Both are frequent Ghost sources and frequent Impact Character/Revenant
    candidates (Stage 4).
  - **Mentors** — a common source for the Flat Archetype casting (the Mentor/Elder/Ruler slots, Stage 4's
    Composability Notes), and directly relevant to any "mentor/mentee bond lost, betrayed, or never had"
    friction point, if the setting's own lore has one.
  - **Siblings or cohort-mates** — relevant to any generational/cohort identity (A1, above), and a common
    source of sibling-dynamic material (who's an ally, who's a competitor for status).
  - **Rivals** — direct input for Four-Corner Opposition (Stage 4) and for casting a same-stage antagonist
    pairing (the Twelve Archetypal Antagonists, Stage 4).
  - **Friends** — direct input for the Village (Stage 4) and for the "Network" delivery channel (Stage 3).
  - **Romantic history**, if any exists prior to the player/protagonist. Feeds the Lie/Flaw node directly for
    romanceable characters via the "I can't love him because ___" quick-fill variant, and is required context
    before the Four Elements of Relationship Sizzle (Stage 4) can be built for her eventual romance arc.
- **Any existing established perks or mechanical hooks tied to her backstory.** Where a character already has
  bespoke mechanics on record, these need to be known before Stage 2 processing starts, since the whole
  processing pass has to remain consistent with mechanics that are already locked, not invent psychology that
  contradicts them.

## A5. Existing Mechanical and Surface Data

Distinguished from the deep psychology layer this methodology produces — but still required as Stage 1 input,
since the deep layer has to remain consistent with what's already mechanically true about her, if the target
project has its own separate mechanical/stat layer at all.

- **A surface/mechanical spec sheet's contents**, if already filled in for this character (see `../Templates/`
  for a real, worked example of what such a sheet can look like).
- **A companion/romance design-inputs sheet's contents**, if already filled in (also in `../Templates/`).
- **Game stats**, if already assigned. A character's psychology should read as plausible given her mechanical
  build — a high-social, low-willpower character implies a different Coping/Defense Mechanism tier (Stage 3)
  than the reverse, whatever the target project's own stat system actually measures. Substitute whatever stat
  system, if any, the target project actually uses.
- **Any already-assigned perks, traits, or skills.** Same reasoning as game stats — the psychology has to be
  consistent with, not contradictory to, mechanics already locked in.
- **Any existing romance-gate design**, if one is already sketched (a stat threshold, a conduct-based gate,
  or an as-yet-undecided approach). Required before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping Table (Stage 5) can be
  fully applied to her, if the target project's own ending-distribution law interacts with romance
  eligibility (see `../Design_Principles/No_Good_Endings.md`).

---

## A6. World-Context Reference Access

Not per-character data — existing setting canon that has to be *accessible* (read, cross-referenced) before
Stage 2's diagnostic tools can meaningfully draw from it. Listed here as a checklist of what kind of material
to have open/available, not material to be newly written per character.

- **Any species/embodiment-specific lore material**, if the setting has more than one kind of being. This
  system's own originating project had a six-chapter reference covering a mortality/integrity threshold, an
  internal-tension mechanism analogous to conscience, trust-bond romance stakes, mentor/mentee bonds,
  locality/belonging, and generational identity — required before any diagnostic technique tied to "what makes
  this kind of being's psychology distinct" can be applied.
- **Local/regional reference material** for her place of origin (and any other location she's meaningfully
  tied to). Required for Background Taxonomy's Environment sub-category, and — per this system's own real
  worked examples — required before the Trigger-Type Design Pattern's actual location-specific triggers can be
  designed at all; a character's local reference material simply not existing yet is a legitimate reason to
  leave that portion of Stage 5 open rather than invent it.
- **Historical-pressure or regional-history material**, if the setting tracks it at that granularity. A common
  source of Ghost material tied to place.
- **Local community/social-infrastructure material**, if it exists. Feeds the Environment sub-category and
  gives concrete texture for the Village (Stage 4).
- **Relevant faction, cultural, or religious canon**, if applicable. Directly required for the Ideas
  sub-category of the Background Taxonomy (Stage 3).
- **Any species/culture-specific consumption, vice, or daily-ritual canon**, if the setting has established
  one (this system's own originating project had a specific robot-culture material set — a robot-specific
  intoxicant, a specific caffeine-equivalent, smoking habits). Feeds the Body sub-category and is directly
  useful raw material for the Mystery Caution's soft-detail delivery mechanism (Stage 2) — what she drinks,
  what her routines look like.
- **The typing system's own reference material** (`../Enneagram/`, if using this Pipeline's default system).
  Required to actually apply the personality-type assignment correctly rather than just naming a type number.

## A7. Standing Design-Law Constraints

Not per-character information — the fixed rules every downstream node's output has to obey, needed as context
before any generative work starts so nothing gets built that later has to be discarded for violating an
existing law. See `../Design_Principles/` for the generalized versions of the rules this system's own
originating project used, and `00_Overall_Process_Scaffold.md`'s Cross-Cutting Constraints section for how
they interact with this Pipeline specifically.

- **A No-Good-Endings / Ending-Distribution law**, if the target project wants one (see
  `../Design_Principles/No_Good_Endings.md`) — minority-negative, majority-bittersweet, costly-positive
  endings. Required before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping Table (Stage 5) can be applied without contradicting
  project canon.
- **A Double-Gate / romance-lock rule**, if the target project wants one — a negative personal-arc ending
  permanently disqualifying romance. Required for the same reason as above.
- **No Demographic Stereotyping.** Directly constrains the Background Taxonomy's Environment and Ideas
  sub-categories, and specifically constrains how the Trigger-Type Design Pattern's location-bound triggers
  get built — any in-fiction reputation a place or people-group carries should be built entirely from invented
  in-fiction material, never mapped onto a real-world demographic stereotype.
- **No Level-Scaling**, if the target project is a game with encounter/loot systems. Not a direct
  character-psychology concern, but a standing constraint on any downstream content this character's arc
  eventually generates.
- **A Dual-Outcome Branching law**, if the target project wants branching character resolutions (see
  `../Design_Principles/Dual_Outcome_Branching.md`) — required before Stage 5's branching beat structure can
  be built, since it's the mechanical skeleton that structure has to serve.
- **Any cross-installment consistency law**, if this character is confirmed to recur across multiple
  installments (see A3, above). Required before Series-Spanning Arc Models (Stage 5) can be applied to her
  specifically.
- **The Standing Scope Note** — every beat is defined by functional role, never by fixed
  percentage-of-runtime placement; this is the entire reason the Midpoint Menu (Stage 5) exists as a menu of
  interchangeable mechanics rather than a single canonical beat.

## A8. The Writer's Own Priming Answers

- **The writer's own answers to the Memory Mining tool's fixed question set** (happiest memory, saddest
  memory, a time of genuine shame, a belief that regularly creates conflict and where it comes from, a time of
  being emotionally hurt) — gathered *before* Stage 2's Self-to-Character Memory Mining technique is run on
  the character. This is explicitly a Stage 1 input-gathering step even though its purpose is to prime Stage
  2's interrogation: the technique requires the self-pass to happen first, to prime honest, non-generic
  answers for the character pass that follows. A one-time cost, not a per-character one — see `00a`.

---

## A9. Existing Narrative Introduction Context

- **Where, when, and how the audience/player first meets her, if this is already planned or already exists.**
  Required before the Characteristic Moment (Stage 4) can be written or audited — you cannot check an
  introduction scene against the Characteristic Moment checklist without knowing what that scene actually is.

---

## A10. Existing Supporting Cast and Relationship Web

- **A named list of every character already established as connected to her** — not just the categorized
  relationships already covered in A4 (family, mentors, rivals, friends), but the full roster of who exists in
  her orbit, regardless of category. Required before Impact Character, Revenant, and Contagonist casting
  (Stage 4) can be done properly, since the strongest version of that casting draws directly from her
  *already-existing* relationships rather than inventing new figures wholesale.

---

## A11. Existing Arc and Ending Concepts

- **Any already-drafted ending ideas, branch concepts, or Dual-Outcome branch outcomes**, even in rough or
  contradictory form. Required before the Ending-Shape Cross-Mapping Table (Stage 5) can be applied, and
  required so that this methodology's own output doesn't silently contradict or duplicate work that already
  exists.
- **Any already-established structural timing facts about her arc**, such as a decision point already locked
  to occur "at the midpoint" or any other named beat. This is exactly the kind of pre-existing structural
  anchor Stage 5 needs to check for before inventing a new one.

---

## A12. Existing Memory and Prior Design Notes

- **A check of any prior design discussion or notes specific to this character**, before starting. A
  character who has already been the subject of design discussion may already have flagged gaps, hypotheses,
  or open questions on record that this pass should build on rather than silently rediscover or contradict.

# SECTION B — OPTIONAL, ENRICHING FIELDS

Nothing below is required by any specific node or tool named in Stages 2 through 5 — the pipeline is fully
runnable without any of it. But each item genuinely enriches the material those nodes and tools produce, and
is worth gathering when available or when time allows.

---

## B1. Soft-Detail Delivery Material

Directly enriches the Mystery Caution (Stage 2, see `../Design_Principles/Mystery_Soft_Detail_Delivery.md`) —
full resolution belongs in private design notes, delivered in actual media only through soft ambient detail,
and this material is exactly what fills that delivery channel.

- **Consumption habits and vice preferences** — favorite drink, food, or equivalent (whatever the setting's
  own culture-specific canon establishes), smoking or equivalent habits if any.
- **Daily routines and small habitual gestures** — the kind of detail (a makeshift shower, scattered beer
  cans, a specific way of handling a piece of equipment) that characterizes without a word of exposition.
- **Clothing and personal style choices**, beyond what's captured in reference art itself — how she wears
  what she owns, day to day.
- **What entertainment she owns or engages with in her own space**, if she has one.

---

## B2. Regional Speech and Dialect Notes

Enriches the Speech-Pattern Correlation Table (Stage 3) beyond its baseline wound-and-self-esteem-driven
correlation — a regional or cultural speech texture layered on top of the psychological correlation table
gives a more specific voice than the correlation table alone would produce.

---

## B3. Existing Reputation and Rumor Material

What other characters, communities, or factions already say about her, if anything is on record — enriches
the "Reputation" delivery channel (Ten Ways, Stage 3) beyond what her own direct actions establish. Not
required, since reputation can be built fresh, but genuinely richer when it already has texture to draw from.

---

## B4. A Pre-Existing Basic Headline

If a single compact behavioral-compass sentence has already organically emerged for this character — normally
this is an *output* of Stage 2/3 processing, but if one already exists as an informal shorthand before formal
processing starts, recording it here saves re-deriving it and can usefully anchor the rest of the pass.

---

## B5. Extended Family Tree and Relationship Web

Beyond the load-bearing relationships already required in A4 and A10 — a fuller extended web (cousins,
acquaintances, professional contacts, casual enemies) enriches the Village (Stage 4) beyond its minimum viable
version, giving more texture to "the diffuse surrounding cast whose accumulated small demands and minor
betrayals carry weight no single major relationship can."

---

## B6. Food and Music Preferences Tied to Origin

Food and music are two areas worth never neglecting when building out a character's geographic/community
background — regional cuisine defines home/hearth and music shapes emotional cueing. Enriches the Background
Taxonomy's Environment sub-category beyond its minimum requirement.

---

## B7. "Tribe" — Specific In-World Group Norms

Distinct from place of origin (which is required, A1) — the specific group whose company she practices in and
whose norms she actually measures herself against, if this differs from or is more specific than her
geographic origin (a particular guild, a particular faction, a particular found-family). Enriches Compare-and-
Contrast Self-Image (Stage 2) by giving it a sharper yardstick than "her home region" alone.

---

## B8. Existing Humor, Wit, or Memorable Lines

Any specimens of her voice already on record, beyond what's captured in A9's introduction-scene material.
Genuinely useful raw material for the Speech-Pattern Correlation Table (Stage 3) and for sympathy-lever
techniques (Stage 4), but not required — voice can be built without prior examples.

---

## B9. Concept Art and Mood-Board References

Beyond the locked reference-art set (required, A1) — additional visual references, mood boards, or aesthetic
touchstones that informed her concept but aren't part of her confirmed reference imagery. Enriches physical
description work without being load-bearing on it.

---

## B10. Secondary Foil and Rival Candidates

Beyond the load-bearing rival relationships already required for Four-Corner Opposition (A4/A10) —
additional, lighter-weight foil candidates who could fill a corner if the primary ones don't work out, or who
could be introduced later without requiring a redesign.

---

## B11. Cross-Media Appearances

If this character appears in, or is referenced by, other properties in the same fictional universe, any notes
or established material from those appearances. Not required for this methodology's own pipeline, but useful
for keeping her psychology consistent across a project's full multimedia footprint.

---

## B12. Prior Playtesting or Informal Feedback

If any exists — reactions, confusion points, or praise already gathered about this specific character from
any informal testing or read-through. Not a formal input any node requires, but useful context for what's
already been shown to land or not land.

---

## B13. The Developer's Own Unformalized Instincts

Aesthetic preferences, conceptual gut-feelings, or "I just want her to feel like X" instincts about this
character that haven't yet been written down as canon anywhere. Explicitly worth capturing even in loose
form — treat this kind of instinct as legitimate, load-bearing input in its own right, not something to
discard until it's been formalized. ("I don't know what sorts of character backstories or situations they
have, but I'm certain that I want them in the game" is itself a complete, usable instinct — see
`../Tepenia_Worked_Examples/` for the real precedent this comes from.)
