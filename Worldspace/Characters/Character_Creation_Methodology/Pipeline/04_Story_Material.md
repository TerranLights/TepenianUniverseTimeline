# Character Development Methodology — Stage 4: Story Material

**Pipeline position:** converting a finished Character Data profile (Stage 3) into actual story scaffolding —
role, supporting cast, thematic shape — before any beat-by-beat work (Stage 5) starts.

**Status:** consolidated, and validated by a full worked example — see `../Tepenia_Worked_Examples/` for the
complete run-through, kept separate so this Pipeline never depends on that specific setting to function. The
worked example validated the stage end-to-end and surfaced one concrete design gap in the process (a missing
antagonist figure) — a real illustration of what running these tools against an atypical character actually
looks like, not just a confirmation of an easy case.

---

## The Front-Gate / Sequencing Trio

Not a merge — three calibration tools answering different questions at different points, run in order:

1. **MICE Quotient** (Milieu / Idea / Character / Event) — asked *first*: what kind of story or sub-thread is
   this? This sets the depth ceiling before any character work starts. A Milieu- or Event-type thread doesn't
   need (and shouldn't be forced to carry) full principal-character-grade characterization.
2. **Character Hierarchy** (walk-on / minor / major) — asked *second*, once MICE confirms "Character" mode
   genuinely applies: how much narrative weight does *this specific* character carry? Walk-ons disappear into
   stereotype on purpose; minor characters get one of three tools (eccentricity, exaggeration, obsessiveness)
   deployed once, briefly; major status is signaled through eight independent, controllable levers
   (strangeness, narrative time, choice-weight, others' Focus on her, frequency, active involvement, audience
   sympathy, POV weight) — not raw characterization depth.
3. **Story Problem vs. Character Problem** — the troubleshooting fallback, run only when something still isn't
   working after 1 and 2 were done correctly: if a character is genuinely necessary and the surrounding story
   is sound, why wouldn't she come alive? If she only exists to mechanically service the plot ("walk in, throw
   a switch, get off"), the fix is structural — replace her narrative function, or admit the story doesn't
   need her to be a full character at all — not more added quirks or backstory.

## Consolidated Node (genuine merge)

**NODE: Contagonist** — merges two independently-named versions of the same function from different craft
traditions (a Dramatica-derived "Contagonist," and a "Counterweight" from a different source). Both name the
identical function: someone close to the protagonist who pulls her backward toward the status quo or an old
trap, in direct tension with whoever's pulling her forward, and — critically, in both sources independently —
**this role does not have to be villainous.** Canonical name: **Contagonist**, since it's the more formally
grounded term, with the "devil-and-angel-on-the-shoulder" framing already attached. One specific nuance folds
in as an attached refinement: the role can simply represent the legitimate emotional pull of tradition,
loyalty, or normalcy, with neither the Contagonist nor whoever she's opposing being clearly "correct."

## Kept Deliberately Separate (a near-merge that turned out not to be one)

**Impact Character** and **Revenant** look, at first pass, like the same function under two names — both are
close, sustained-proximity figures who catalyze a character's inner change. On closer inspection they're
defined by genuinely different mechanisms, and merging them loses real information:

- **Impact Character** is defined by **worldview contrast** — she embodies the Truth, and the friction against
  the protagonist's Lie comes from opposing worldviews, not from mirroring anything in the protagonist's past.
  This function can be filled by a collective, not just an individual (the classic example: an entire small
  town, functioning as a single Impact Character).
- **Revenant** is defined by **recurrence** — she's specifically whoever forces the character to confront
  "that *same* unresolved problem *again*, now," a present-day echo of the original Ghost. The Revenant should
  almost always be an actual person, not a diffuse situation or group — a direct boundary-condition conflict
  with the collective-Impact-Character option above, not just a difference in emphasis.

A Revenant doesn't have to be Truth-aligned to do her job — she can simply be a new source of the same
pressure the original wound came from, without herself modeling anything healthier (a new authority figure
making the identical demand the character's original abuser made, for instance). **This is exactly where
Revenant shades toward antagonist-adjacent territory** that belongs to the villain/anti-hero supplement
material (see `../Source_Material/Character_Development_Methodology_-_Villains_and_Antiheroes_-_DRAFT_Ideas.md`)
rather than this file — a non-Truth-aligned Revenant is functionally closer to a Contagonist or an outright
antagonist than to an Impact Character, and should be built using that material's own tools (a Predatory
Villain subtype, a Sadist/Bully distinction) when that's the shape she's taking.

**Practical use:** treat these as two separate, composable slots, not one function with two names.
- The *strongest* single-character version does both jobs at once — this role is "often most powerful when
  the character also helps with the protagonist's external, plot-level goal," which is exactly the
  convergence case where a Revenant also happens to embody the Truth.
- They can also legitimately split across two different characters (a Revenant who tests without teaching,
  and a separate Impact Character who teaches without any connection to the original wound's specific shape)
  — or one can be present without the other (a Truth-embodying collective Impact Character with no single
  Revenant at all).

## The Player-Necessity Rule and Related Constraints

**Fully optional — a real, concrete example of what it looks like to adapt this Stage into a companion-driven
game system**, kept here because it's genuinely useful precedent, not because every project needs it.
Synthesized from one specific project's already-established, battle-tested "Personal Questline Design Rule"
and a read-through of the actual character-arc files already built on it. It sharpens and mechanizes the
Impact Character/Revenant material immediately above into an explicit, checkable design rule with real teeth
— worth adapting if your own project has playable-character-driven companion arcs; safe to skip entirely
otherwise.

- **The core rule**: every companion's personal arc should hinge on something the *player specifically* can do
  that the companion cannot — not because she's generally incapable, but because of a genuine categorical
  exclusion (wrong architecture, no access, barred by her own history or institutional position). The
  companion's own resources — competence, faction, connections — get her most of the way; the last, personal
  step has to be something only the player could have done for her. Without this, the player is reduced to a
  bystander watching the companion's own faction or skills solve her problem off-screen.
- **The categorical-block sanity check**: confirm the exclusion is real before designing how the player
  succeeds at it. **If a companion could in principle also clear the threshold given enough time or luck, the
  "exclusion" isn't one — it's just the player being better at something she could also, eventually, do
  herself, which collapses the whole rule.** Run this check explicitly before treating any categorical block
  as settled. (See `../Tepenia_Worked_Examples/` for a concrete worked instance of this check holding up under
  scrutiny.)
- **The compounding-reasons technique**: a single categorical reason doesn't have to carry the whole weight
  alone. A block can stack multiple independent reasons — dividing focus risks the recovery itself,
  confronting the pressure openly would tip off exactly what she's trying to keep hidden, and doing so would
  destroy institutional credibility every one of her possible endings depends on, for instance. When one
  reason feels thin on its own, look for a second and third that independently support the same exclusion
  rather than forcing a single weak justification to do all the work.
- **The no-escort-quest constraint** (a binding structural rule worth adopting as a default): cost, difficulty,
  and vulnerability fall on the *player*, never on the companion as an NPC who has to be kept alive through
  the mechanism that resolves her own arc. Worth treating as a standing constraint on any personal-arc
  mechanism this stage produces, not just a stylistic preference.
- **Retrofit discipline**: when revising an already-built arc's mechanism rather than designing one fresh,
  scope the change to exactly what needs fixing and say explicitly what's *not* being touched. A targeted
  retrofit should replace only the antagonist/access mechanism while leaving already-locked material (an
  existing ending set, an existing branch structure) untouched and explicitly noted as such — this stops a
  targeted fix from silently drifting into an unplanned full redesign.

## Standalone Tools

- **The Normal World as symbolic dramatization of the Lie** — the opening world/circumstances actively built
  to reinforce the Lie, not just contain the character; the contrast with wherever her arc takes her should be
  maximized, not softened.
- **The Characteristic Moment** — the master first-scene checklist, absorbing two related, narrower
  techniques:
  - The **Four-Fact Opening** — reveal a minimum of ~4 distinct facts about her, spread across different
    categories (physical, personal, social, familial, societal, hobby), within her first meaningful scene,
    rather than concentrated in one kind of detail or delivered as exposition.
  - The **Strength/Longing/Fear staging rule** — establish all three quickly, in the same early scene(s),
    rather than leading with wound/flaw alone; Fear specifically should be *hinted*, not fully displayed, in
    an opening (save its full weight for the low point in Stage 5).
- **Antagonist vs. Antagonistic Force** — the concrete-agent vs. abstract-obstacle distinction, worth naming
  both deliberately for any significant antagonist (a dragon as antagonist standing in for the antagonistic
  force of "stagnation," for instance).
- **The Twelve Archetypal Antagonists** (six outer/inner pairs mapped to six Life Arcs) — a pre-built content
  library for giving a character's arc a concretely-shaped opponent, including the forward-looking technique
  of drawing an antagonist from the *next* arc-stage's aggressive shadow rather than the protagonist's own
  stage. **When more than one antagonist candidate, or more than one Impact Character/Revenant casting option,
  was seriously considered before one was chosen, keep the runners-up written down** rather than letting the
  comparison itself be the only record of them — see `../Design_Principles/Preserve_Unchosen_Material.md`.
- **Four-Corner Opposition & the Character Web** — the general-purpose roster/rival design method: a character
  plus three others pushed to the corners, each attacking her core weakness differently, each with a distinct
  cluster of values in conflict with the others'.
- **The Four Elements of Relationship Sizzle** — a dedicated pair-design checklist: something genuinely in
  common, a real conflict, contrasting qualities, and mutual-transformation potential.
- **A functional-role catalogue for supporting cast** — Crucial Ally (including the "remorseless sidekick"
  sub-pattern, who performs morally compromising violence so the protagonist can retain a clean moral center),
  Betrayer and Sympathetic Heavy (a betrayal only lands if real trust was established first; the Sympathetic
  Heavy is its inverse — aligned with the opponent but risking exposure to help the protagonist anyway),
  Visitor and Stranger (an outside-perspective character who makes an established setting feel freshly
  strange), and the Village (the diffuse surrounding cast whose accumulated small demands and minor betrayals
  carry weight no single major relationship can).

## Composability Notes (orthogonal systems — cross-reference, not merge)

- **The functional-role catalogue × the Six Flat Archetypes.** A character's *function* (Crucial Ally,
  Contagonist, Betrayer, etc.) and her *life-stage content* (Child/Lover/Parent/Ruler/Elder/Mentor) are
  independent axes, not competing systems — any functional role can be cast using any Flat Archetype's
  content. A Mentor-stage Crucial Ally reads differently from a Parent-stage one, and both are valid.
- **Four-Corner Opposition × the Twelve Archetypal Antagonists.** The general method (any four characters,
  pushed to the corners) and the pre-built content library (specific named pairs with ready-made thematic
  freight) aren't competing — the pre-built pairs can fill one or two of the Four-Corner "corners," with the
  remainder built via the general method when nothing pre-built fits.

## Two Staging/Sympathy Techniques Worth Keeping Distinct

- **A general sympathy-lever catalogue** (the non-villain half of "Characters We Love/Characters We Hate") —
  Altruism calibrated by role (Victim/Savior/Sacrifice, each with a specific way to misfire), visible Plan and
  Purpose, Courage paired with Fair Play, the **Draftee-or-Volunteer rule** (volunteering for a thankless task
  earns sympathy, being forced into it reads as weak — and the reverse for a glory-bringing task),
  Dependability (a costly kept promise is one of the strongest reusable sympathy engines in fiction),
  Cleverness without displayed Intelligence, and Endearing Imperfections. The antagonist-specific half (Sadist/
  Bully, Self-Appointed/Usurper Rule, Assassin-vs-Avenger, Understandable Villain) belongs to the villain/anti-
  hero supplement material rather than here.
- **Cat-save and delay-the-worst-act staging techniques** — an early, authentic act of unearned kindness near
  a character's first appearance, and holding back a morally compromising act until the audience already has
  real investment in her, are both staging decisions about *when in the story material* certain beats land,
  not character-data fields.
