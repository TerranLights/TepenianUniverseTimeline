# Character Spec — Fill-In Sheet Template

**What this is:** a plain-language intake worksheet — a set of questions with a short rationale under each —
that feeds the Pipeline's Stage 1 (`../Pipeline/01_Input_Information.md`). Fill in whatever you have a clear
sense of under "Your answer," and leave the rest as `TBD`. Nothing here needs to be answered all at once, and
nothing here needs to be answered in order.

**How this maps onto a project's own file structure:** this sheet's sections are meant to feed directly into
whatever folder skeleton a project keeps per character (a profile document, a background/history file, a
relationships file, a timeline, and — if she turns out to be a recruitable/major character — a personal-
arc-design file). You don't need to write in that structured format yourself; write in plain prose here, in
your own words, and the structured files get built from your answers afterward.

**Where to put it:** copy this file into the character's own folder once you're ready to start developing her.
Not every section applies to every character — a background-only or off-screen character, for instance, may
not need Section 12 (Role in the Story) filled out in any depth.

**If you don't have a direct answer to a section, here's what to give instead.** None of the sections below
require you to already have a finished answer. If a section stalls you out, any of the following raw material
works just as well:

- **A comparison.** A real person you know, an existing fictional character, or another character already in
  the project — "she's like X, but Y." A comparison plus one point of difference is usually enough to
  pattern-match the rest.
- **A single scene or image, however small.** Not a plot — just one moment you can picture her in: something
  she says, something she's doing, how she reacts to one specific thing. Often enough to reverse-engineer a
  much larger answer from.
- **What you don't want.** Ruling something out ("I don't want her to be a generic tsundere," "I don't want
  her weakness to be that she's 'too nice'") narrows the space just as effectively as picking something.
- **Tone/vibe words.** A handful of adjectives for how she should feel overall — wry, quietly tragic, warm,
  unsettling — even before any specifics exist.
- **Whatever's already written about her**, even fragments — an existing profile stub, her reference art, a
  half-formed note anywhere else. Mine existing material directly rather than restating it here.

## 4. Personality, in Your Own Words

Don't worry about any personality-typing terminology or framework — just describe her the way you'd describe
someone you know: what she's like, what she cares about, what irritates her, what she avoids, how she'd react
under pressure. Adjectives, a comparison to another character, a specific behavior you picture her doing —
anything concrete helps more than an abstract label. A matching personality-type assignment and any mechanical
baseline should be proposed *from* this description, not the other way around.

While you're in here, four more specific things worth including if you have a sense of them:
- **Her fundamental flaw or weakness** — not a cute quirk, an actual liability that can cause her real
  problems.
- **What she's genuinely good at** — skills or strengths, practical or otherwise.
- **What she values most** — the people, ideals, or things she cares about above everything else.
- **Any internal contradiction** — two things she wants that can't both be true at once.

## 14. Character Craft Notes

A few reflective, meta-level questions — useful for keeping the overall cast varied and intentional, not
required for her to function as a character:
- **Inspiration mix** — roughly how much of her comes from a real person you know, pure imagination, a famous
  figure or literary character, versus just intuitive "this feels right"? A rough split is fine.
- **Round or flat, on purpose** — is she meant to be multidimensional and evolving, or functional and single-
  note by design? Flat isn't a flaw here — it can be the right call for a background, comic-relief, or pure
  world-building role.
- **Anti-stereotype check** — is she deliberately subverting a stereotype some part of her design might
  otherwise invite? (See `../Design_Principles/` for a standing rule against demographic stereotyping worth
  adopting as a default — this is a natural place to flag if a given origin or background needs a deliberate
  counter-note.)
