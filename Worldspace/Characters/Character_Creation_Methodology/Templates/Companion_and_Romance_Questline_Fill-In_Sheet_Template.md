# Companion & Romance Questline — Fill-In Sheet Template

**What this is:** the second-stage worksheet, for once a character's core identity (per
`Character_Spec_Fill-In_Sheet_Template.md`) is established. That sheet covers who she is; this one covers how
her personal arc and her romance (if any) actually work. Genuinely optional — only relevant for a
project with a companion/romance system to design against at all.

**Why the sections below are fixed, not open brainstorming:** if your project already has binding design law
governing every companion and romance arc (see `../Design_Principles/`), the sections below aren't a menu of
ideas — they're the raw creative material those fixed systems need in order to function for *this specific*
character. You don't need to know any of the underlying mechanics to answer them; write in plain language, the
same way Section 4 of the Character Spec sheet doesn't require knowing what a personality-typing system is.
Whoever's building her mechanical shape can translate the answers into the actual mechanical form (branch
count, exact numbers, names, etc.).

**Sequencing:** fill in `Character_Spec_Fill-In_Sheet_Template.md` first if you haven't already — her
personality, goal, values, and internal conflict from that sheet are exactly what the questions below draw
from. This sheet is Part 2, not a replacement.

**These fields ask for conclusions, and you're not expected to hand over finished conclusions.** A core
conflict, a capability gap, a courtship sequence — those are things that should be derivable, not things you
need to invent from scratch. Here's what to give instead, roughly in order of how much leverage each one
provides:

1. **A well-filled-out Character Spec Sheet — the biggest lever by far.** If her weakness, goal, values,
   internal conflict, origin, current status, and relationships are already written down, first-pass answers
   to most of Part A and Part B below should be draftable directly, because those things are supposed to fall
   out of who she already is rather than get invented separately. Fill that sheet in reasonably well first.
2. **A comparison.** A real person, an existing fictional character, or another character already in the
   project — "she's like X, but Y." A comparison plus one point of difference is very efficient raw material.
3. **A single scene or image, however small.** Not a plot — one moment you can picture her in. Many real
   characters trace back to exactly one vivid scene or single-sentence idea rather than a fully reasoned-out
   backstory.
4. **What you don't want.** Ruling something out ("I don't want the romance to be slow-burn," "I don't want
   her weakness to be that she's 'too nice'") narrows the space just as effectively as picking something.
5. **Tone/vibe words.** A handful of adjectives for how her story should feel — bittersweet, wry, triumphant-
   but-costly, uncomfortable-in-a-good-way — shapes the register of the questions below even before any
   specifics exist.
6. **Whatever's already written about her**, even fragments — an existing profile stub, her reference art, a
   half-formed note anywhere else.

**Use this space for any of items 2-6 above** (item 1 just means: go fill in the Character Spec Sheet — no
separate space needed for it here). None of these are required, and there's no wrong way to fill them in —
loose, informal notes are exactly what this space is for.

### A2. What Only the Player Can Do For Her

Somewhere in resolving A1, there needs to be a final step that she genuinely cannot take herself — not because
she's incompetent, but because something about her nature, access, role, or history structurally excludes her
from it. Her own resources should get her most of the way there; think about what specifically stops her short
of the finish line, and what it is about the player/protagonist that clears that specific obstacle. (See
`../Pipeline/04_Story_Material.md`'s Player-Necessity Rule for the full generalized version of this

### B1. What She's Attracted To, and Why

Based on her personality and history, what draws her to a partner — physical presence, intelligence and wit,
courage, genuine emotional depth, perceptiveness, sheer capability and endurance? More than one is fine. If

the answer should come from who she is, not from game balance.
