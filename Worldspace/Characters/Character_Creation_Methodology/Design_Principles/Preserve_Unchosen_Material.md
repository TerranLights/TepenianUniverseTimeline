# Design Principle — Preserve Unchosen Material

**What this is:** a standing cross-cutting discipline, not scoped to one stage. First formalized as the
Overflow-file mechanic inside Stage 5's Arc-Space Enumeration (see
`../Pipeline/05_Beats_Paths_Results.md`) — where a character's unselected candidate arcs get written down in
full rather than discarded — then generalized here, because the same logic applies at every stage of this
Pipeline that generates more than one concrete candidate and only carries one forward.

---

## The Rule

**Any time a stage in this Pipeline produces more than one concrete, character-specific candidate — a
psychological hypothesis, a casting choice, a full arc, a backstory detail, an antagonist pairing — and only
one gets selected to move forward, the rest don't get discarded.** They get written down somewhere durable and
discoverable (a per-character "Overflow" or "Alternates" file, a clearly-labeled section in her own design
notes, whatever file convention the target project already uses) at the same level of detail they were
actually developed to — not compressed into a one-line footnote, not left only in a chat transcript or a
deleted draft.

## Why This Matters

A candidate that loses out to a better fit for *this* character, in *this* context, isn't necessarily a bad
idea — it's an idea that didn't win one specific comparison. The bar for "worth preserving" is much lower than
the bar for "worth using right now": a rejected Lie hypothesis for one character might be exactly right for a
different character built later from a similar seed; a rejected antagonist candidate might resurface for a
sequel or an expansion; a discarded Life Arc archetype fit might become the basis of an entirely different
character down the line. None of that potential value can be recovered later if the material was never
written down in the first place — a decision made once, silently, becomes a decision no one downstream even
knows was made.

## Where This Shows Up Across the Pipeline

Not an exhaustive list — a pointer to where this discipline is most likely to matter at each stage:

- **Stage 2** (`../Pipeline/02_Information_Processing.md`) — the Scenario Diagnostic Template generates 4-6
  genuinely different psychological answer options per decision point; whichever ones don't end up matching
  this character are still fully-reasoned character studies in their own right, worth keeping.
- **Stage 3** (`../Pipeline/03_Character_Data.md`) — when more than one Life Arc archetype or more than one
  Lie/Truth hypothesis was genuinely considered before one was selected as the best fit, the runners-up are
  real alternate readings of the same raw material, not wrong answers.
- **Stage 4** (`../Pipeline/04_Story_Material.md`) — alternate antagonist candidates from the Twelve
  Archetypal Antagonists library, or alternate Impact Character/Revenant casting options, that were seriously
  considered but not used.
- **Stage 5** (`../Pipeline/05_Beats_Paths_Results.md`) — the fullest worked instance of this rule: Arc-Space
  Enumeration's Overflow file, capturing every plausible candidate arc that didn't get selected for a linear
  project's single collapsed arc.

## Practical Scope — Don't Over-Apply This

This isn't a mandate to archive every rejected sentence or first-draft phrasing that got improved two minutes
later — that would bury the genuinely reusable material under noise instead of protecting it. Scope this to
real forks in the creative process: a fully-reasoned candidate that was seriously in contention, not
throwaway phrasing on the way to a better version of the same idea. If a candidate was never developed far
enough to actually judge, there's nothing yet worth preserving — develop it first, or don't bother.

## Who Decides What Gets Carried Forward

The same convention established for Stage 5's collapse step applies at every stage's own selection points, not
just the final one: **whoever or whatever is applying this methodology proposes the strongest candidate and
states plainly why, but the developer is the one who actually confirms the choice.** Nothing here should be
narrowed down unilaterally and silently — proposing a best fit and preserving the rest are two halves of the
same discipline, not competing instincts.
