# No National Stereotypes — A Binding Worldbuilding Rule

**Status:** Confirmed, binding — applies across every Tepenian Universe project (games, novels, TV)

**The rule:** a Tepenian city's real-world station-builder nation (which country originally established the underlying Antarctic research station) is a GPS coordinate only. It locates the city on the map. It must never be used as a reason, cause, or explanation for anything else in the fiction — not a city's present-day culture, not a faction's internal conflict, not a character's temperament, not which real-world city makes a good creative inspiration, not why a war strike hit one city and not another.

**Self-check:** if a sentence's meaning would change were the station-builder nation swapped for a different one, that sentence violates this rule.

**Who founded each city is a separate question, answered only by the Founding Register** (`/home/kuroskalacs/Documents/Doll-Fi/media/games/Inner Tepenia/InnerTepeniaGDD/Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Outside-World/Tepenian-Federation/Locations/Cities/Founding_Register.md`). A city's founders stand on geography and access — time-zone proximity and practical reach — never on the station (`DR-19`).

**The scale of time that makes this rule necessary, not just a style preference:** the Falkland Treaty was signed **June 21, 2564.** William Shakespeare was born April 23, 1564. **We are, today, closer to the birth of William Shakespeare than we are to the signing of the Falkland Treaty** — and closer to the publication of the King James Bible (1611) too. Expecting a Tepenian city's present-day culture to still resemble its station-builder nation's real-world stereotypes is exactly as implausible as expecting modern English culture to still resemble Shakespeare's England just because Shakespeare was English. That much elapsed time makes a station-builder nation pure geography — a GPS coordinate marking where something started, never a live causal force on what a culture looks like on the far side of that gap.

**Sharper still, confirmed 2026-07-24: the founding generation itself had zero living connection, not just present-day Tepenians.** Real-world Antarctic research stations mostly date to the International Geophysical Year (1957-58); even the oldest continuously-run station (Argentina's Orcadas, 1904) is still roughly 600-660 years short of the Falkland Treaty. **The station's real-world national origin was already ancient history on day one of Tepenia's own existence** — the 2564 exiles who actually signed the treaty had no living connection to whichever national team built the station they arrived at, before a single day of independent Tepenian culture had happened yet. **Direct consequence:** a city's own founding-history narrative should never read as if the real-world station's construction and Tepenia's founding happened in causal or temporal proximity. This rules out more than culture-as-inheritance: it rules out **real station personnel from the 1950s-70s still being alive, present, or personally interacting with the 2564 founding exiles** — a distinct, more literal violation than a stereotype.

**Refined 2026-07-25 — the mechanism for *most* stations is rotating hand-off maintenance across the First Interwar Period (2083-2564), not centuries of dormancy.** An earlier framing ("sat dormant, unstaffed, for centuries") solved the institutional-continuity problem but created a physical-plausibility one — several founding cities' own stories depend on inheriting genuinely *functional*, well-maintained infrastructure, which a centuries-derelict shell doesn't support. **The actual mechanism:** most stations were continuously maintained by a rotating succession of different national operators, handed off from one to another over five centuries — **which specific countries held a given station at any point in that chain is explicitly irrelevant and should never be specified.** This keeps GPS-purposes-only fully intact (no single institution's culture, personnel, or continuity persists — the point is that it changed hands repeatedly) while explaining why the infrastructure was still functional when the 2564 exiles arrived. **Avoid a circular trap:** never have the rotation conveniently end back with the station's original nation — if the chain is genuinely unspecified and insignificant, that convenient ending does no real work and isn't a coincidence worth writing. The rotation's only job is explaining functional infrastructure across the gap — **never** re-deriving which nation a city's founding population turns out to be (that's established separately, per city, on geography and access, in the Founding Register).

**Two deliberate exceptions, not defaults:**
- **Byrd** actually fell out of the maintenance chain and was abandoned — exactly why it's rare enough to be discoverable much later, distinct from the normal (maintained-throughout) case every other founding city follows.
- **Zhongshan** — given the Sinian Federation's immediate geographic and time-zone access to the site (`DR-21`) — plausibly stayed under continuous Chinese/Sinian habitation throughout the First Interwar Period rather than passing through rotating operators. This is generational *habitation continuity* (a population, naturally evolving over five centuries), not the *institutional-culture* continuity the rule forbids. The Jeju-do court later confirmed the claim. Only reach for an exception like this where a city already has its own separate, established reason to be a special case.

**Records, research, results, equipment and techniques can cross the gap; personhood cannot.** Whether a station passed through rotating operators or (Zhongshan's case) one continuous population, the journals, logs, orientation manuals, research data and results, equipment, and the techniques written into them are inheritable (`DR-25`, `DR-26`) — exiles can plausibly find, learn from and carry on that material without being taught by a living institution or person. A **tradition** cannot cross: it passes person to person, and no one carried it across the gap. Learning from a centuries-old written record is categorically different from being personally instructed by surviving personnel, and doesn't violate this rule the way the latter would.

Any operational skill or discipline in a founding-era story should be sourced from **skilled exiles among the founding population itself** — people with real institutional backgrounds of their own era, exiled in 2564 — or **rediscovered from the station's records, research and equipment** by the newcomers (`DR-25`). Never tie those skills to the station's own program, and never source them from the station's personnel bridging a 600-plus-year gap.

# ⛔⛔⛔ READ THIS BEFORE APPLYING THE RULE — **"FOUNDING NATION" WAS AMBIGUOUS, AND THE AMBIGUITY CAUSED REAL OVER-APPLICATION**

**Added 2026-09-06, at the developer's direction, after a live location pass refused ALL ethnic material for
eleven consecutive work pieces on a city whose population composition had been settled for two months.**

> ## **THIS RULE GOVERNS THE STATION'S BUILDER. IT DOES NOT GOVERN THE EXILE POPULATION'S ORIGIN. THOSE ARE TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. WHERE THEY NAME THE SAME NATION, THAT COMES FROM GEOGRAPHY AND ACCESS, NEVER FROM THE STATION.**

**The rule's own opening says so — *"which country originally established the underlying Antarctic research
station"* — but the phrase "founding nation" reads naturally as "the nation that founded the city," which is
the exile population. This file therefore says "station-builder nation" for the first.** ***A reader who takes it the second way will forbid exactly the material this rule was
never aimed at.***

| | **STATION-BUILDER NATION** | **EXILE-POPULATION ORIGIN** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | *who physically built the research station, centuries before the Falkland Treaty* | ⭐ **who actually settled the city in 2564 and lives there** |
| **Established by** | real-world history | ***the Founding Register, on geography and access (`DR-19`); the census gives the composition*** |
| **Status** | ⛔⛔ **GPS COORDINATE ONLY. Everything in this document applies, unchanged** | ✅ **ADMISSIBLE — see the sequencing rule below** |
| **Worked example** | **Shirayuki sits on the Bharati site.** ⛔ *No Indian or South Asian anything, ever — see [[No_Subcontinentals]], a separate binding rule that no South Asian population ever came to Tepenia at all* | ⭐ **Shirayuki was allocated to JAPAN by the Jeju-do court and is Japan-founded.** ✅ *That is usable* |
| **Its neighbors** | Sinheung sits on the Progress site; Zhongshan on the Zhongshan site | **Sinheung is Korea-founded; Zhongshan is China-founded** |

> ### ⭐⭐ **THE PURPOSE OF THE RULE, STATED BY THE DEVELOPER 2026-09-06:**
> > ***"You shall NOT incorporate 'Russian heritage' into the establishment of the city of Shirayuki (or
> > Sinheung, whichever). Once the population composition has [been] established, then, yes, you can start
> > incorporating aspects of Japanese (or Korean) culture, traditions, history, social norms, etc., because
> > Shirayuki is a fundamentally Japanese city (or Sinheung, being a fundamentally Korean city). **THAT'S the
> > purpose of the 'GPS purposes only' Law.**"***
>
> ## ***THE LAW EXISTS SO THAT A CITY IS CHARACTERIZED BY WHO LIVES THERE, RATHER THAN BY WHOSE STATION IT OCCUPIES.***
> **It is a rule about PROVENANCE. It is not a rule against ethnicity.**
> ⚠ ***A pass that refuses all ethnic material is not obeying this law — it is misreading it, and it will
> produce placeless cities.***

## ⭐ THE SEQUENCING RULE

> ***"Once the various nationality-origins, and their proportionate-percentage compositions, have been
> established for some particular place/location/setting/society, THEN it's acceptable to take into
> consideration their origin-ethnicities, ethnic-cultures, etc."***

| Stage | Permitted |
|---|---|
| **BEFORE composition is established** | ⛔ **You may not infer WHO LIVES SOMEWHERE from national character, temperament or cultural reputation.** *This is the heart of the prohibition and it is untouched* |
| ⭐ **AFTER composition is established** | ✅ **Origin-ethnicities and ethnic-cultures may be taken into consideration.** *The populations are canon; what they carry is then in scope* |

# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE DIVERGENCE PRINCIPLE — **composition names the STOCK; time and place produce the CULTURE**

**And this is what item 4 below has always been reaching for. It is not "build culture from nothing."**

> ***"People (whether human or robot), after enough generations, will develop into new, unique cultures which
> are, to an extent, shaped by their surroundings, their hardships and struggles, their common life
> objectives."***
>
> ***"This is one of the major causal factors why 'Québécois' culture is a unique phenomenon, distinct from
> the culture of France. Why 'Canadian' culture is… distinct from the culture of the Celtic lands. Why
> 'American' culture is… distinct from the culture of Germany and the Netherlands. Why 'Australian' culture
> is… distinct from the culture of England. Why 'Singaporean' culture is… distinct from the culture of
> China."***

| ⛔ Too little | ⛔ Too much | ✅ The operation |
|---|---|---|
| **refusing all ethnic material** → *placeless cities* | **transplanting the source culture intact** → *a costumed version of somewhere real* | ⭐⭐ ***take the origin culture as the STARTING STOCK, then apply the local divergence to it*** |

**THE OPERATOR'S INPUTS — the developer's own list, and it is the checklist:**
### **time · separation · local environmental setting · local struggles and hardships · local goals · local sensibilities and habits**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ AND IT IS AN ANTI-CONVERGENCE ENGINE, NOT A LICENSE TO CONVERGE
> ***Two cities founded from the same stock MUST diverge, because the operator's inputs differ.***
> **Worked case: Argentina founded three Tepenian cities — Marambio, Esperanza and Belgrano (Founding Register).**
> *Same stock, same elapsed time, different local conditions and demographic outcomes.* **If a pass produces
> similar-shaped Argentine-descended cultures for all three, at least one is wrong.**

# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE GRAND-TIMELINE ACTS — **when the divergence actually happened**

| Act | Roughly | What the people ARE |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **ACT 1** | **2564 → somewhere in the 2600s** *(a hazy range)* | ***"Japanese / Chinese / Russian / American / Mexican who live in Antarctica."*** **Origin cultures still FRESH** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **ACT 2** | **by the late 2600s / early 2700s, onward** | ## ***Properly TEPENIAN — in name and in cultural identity.*** **Origin is now ANCESTRY, not identity** |

> ***"…similar to the way that by the early-1800s, a person from Pennsylvania and a person from Virginia may
> very well have very different ethnic backgrounds and local cultures — they were both still fundamentally
> AMERICAN."***

⚠ **The Second Interwar Period (2564–2812) SPANS BOTH ACTS and is mostly Act 2.** *Act 1 is its earlier part; the
exact boundary is a hazy range, to be dated after the new vignettes are written and placed in order along the
timeline (developer, 2026-10-01).* ⛔ ***A story that writes its city as its founding nation is writing the wrong
Act for most of those 248 years.***

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE MECHANISM: **INTERCONNECTION — and Amundsen Tower's COMPLETION solidified it**

> ***"That 'metacultural unification' was ONE OF (among multitudinous other) effects of the completion of
> Amundsen Tower. Their being fully interconnected further defined their sense of national identity in who
> they are as a nation of people (both robots and humans alike)."***

### ⛔ FOUR QUALIFICATIONS THAT MUST NOT BE DROPPED

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | ⛔ **NOT a switch.** *"The metacultural unification didn't happen overnight. It was already IN PROGRESS by the time construction of the Tower even started"* |
| **2** | ⛔⛔ **NOT the creation of unity.** ***"Before the completion of Amundsen Tower, the people naturally had a sense of shared struggles and shared goals in a shared country. They didn't consider themselves 'psychologically/culturally/spiritually separate' from each other"*** |
| **3** | ⛔ **NOT the Tower's only effect** — *"one of, among multitudinous other."* **The Tower is primarily energy regulation. Do not write it as the cause of everything** |
| **4** | ⭐ **What it DID:** *full interconnection, which **further defined** and was **"the decisive force that helped solidify their sense of national identity."***  |

> ### ⭐⭐ SO THE ACT BOUNDARY IS NOT A DATE — IT IS A CONSOLIDATION.
> **The Tower completes ~2688: the decisive moment inside a transition already in progress.** ***Write it as a
> gradient with a decisive moment inside it, never as a line crossed.***

## ⭐ THE CORRECT FORM OF WORDS

⛔ **"Shirayuki is a Japanese city"** — *true of Act 1; misleading for most of the period.*
✅ ⭐ **"A Japan-FOUNDED TEPENIAN city."** ***Stock: Japanese. People: Tepenian. Local culture: its own.***

> ### ⚠ AND WHERE TO DIFFERENTIATE — **the Pennsylvania/Virginia test**
> | ✅ Differentiate HERE | ⛔ NOT here |
> |---|---|
> | **local culture, local conditions, local struggles, local habits** | **national identity** — *every Tepenian city is Tepenian; that is not a differentiator* |

## Every way this has been violated before (and corrected, in InnerTepeniaGDD)

1. Explaining a city's present-day culture or a faction's internal conflict via station-builder-nation stereotype ("German rigor," "Russian dusha") instead of the city's own independently-developed identity.
2. A bare "Being [Nationality]." or "[Nationality] Tepenian city" identity claim in a summary line.
3. Picking, rejecting, or even neutrally mentioning whether a real-world creative-inspiration pick shares a nationality with a city's station-builder nation — even a defensive "no overlap, no issue" note is itself a violation.
4. Framing an established in-world cultural practice (an institution, cuisine, music, art) as inherited from or carried over from the founding real-world nation, rather than developed independently by the exile community. A practice may echo a real-world tradition as coincidence; it must never be explained by that tradition.
   > ⚠⚠ **DISAMBIGUATED 2026-09-06 — "the founding real-world nation" here means THE STATION'S BUILDER, and this item is unchanged with respect to it.** ⛔ **It does NOT mean the exile population's own origin.** ⭐ **A practice inherited from the *exile community's own* origin stock is ADMISSIBLE once composition is established — and must then have the divergence operator applied to it, because after ~250 years and the Act 1 → Act 2 shift it is a Tepenian practice descended from that stock, not the stock itself.** *See the disambiguation section above.*
5. Using a city's station-builder nation as an explanatory factor in an in-world strategic, military, or narrative decision (e.g. "Upper Earth struck City X, not City Y, because X's station-builder nation gave it cleaner symbolic legibility as a target").
6. Writing a city's founding-history narrative as if the real-world station's original national personnel, command structure, or institutional continuity survived the 600-plus-year gap to the 2564 exiles — e.g. describing an "Air Force institutional character from the founding era" as if it passed down from the actual 1950s-70s Air Force rather than being independently re-established by skilled exiles among the 2564 arrivals, or rediscovered from the station's records.

## How to apply

- The station-builder nation can still appear as a bare fact about the physical infrastructure the exiles inherited ("the station was built by X in 19XX", `DR-24`), and a city may keep the station's name, or a name honoring a specific historical figure, as a naming fact only — never the source of the city's identity (`DR-28`). **If no figure or fact is immediately apparent, don't force one: nothing is better than something wrong presented as fact.** None of these can ever be the *reason* something else in the fiction is true.
- When writing a city's culture, institutions, cuisine, music, or art, build it from the exile community's own circumstances (isolation, density, terrain, founding-era pressures) — never from **the station-builder** nation's real-world culture.
  > ⭐⭐ **AMENDED 2026-09-06.** ***"The exile community's own circumstances" does not mean "from nothing."***
  > **Build it from: the exile community's own ORIGIN STOCK** *(admissible once composition is established)*
  > **PLUS the local circumstances listed here** *(isolation, density, terrain, founding-era pressures)*
  > **PLUS the divergence operator** *(time · separation · environment · struggles · goals · habits)*.
  > ⛔ **What is forbidden is sourcing it from whoever BUILT THE STATION.** ⚠ *And the output is a Tepenian
  > culture descended from that stock — not the stock transplanted, and not a culture from nowhere.*
- When choosing real-world creative inspirations for a city, judge them entirely on structural/thematic parallel — never mention whether they share or don't share a nationality with the city's station-builder nation, in either direction (even a defensive disclaimer that there's no overlap is itself the mistake).
- When explaining a plot event (why a strike hit one city and not another, why a faction formed where it did), build the explanation from the city's own established facts — population, geography, infrastructure, civic role, timeline — never from its station-builder nation.
- When writing a city's founding-era history, infrastructure survives to 2564 via a rotating, unspecified succession of national operators (never name or dwell on which nations) — or, for Zhongshan specifically, continuous single-population habitation. Either way, it can never hand the exiles institutional knowledge, personnel, or command continuity. If a founding story needs someone with real operational expertise, source it from **skilled exiles among the 2564 founding population** (people with real institutional backgrounds of their own era, never tied to the station's program) — and the station's records, research, results, equipment and techniques can also cross the gap (`DR-25`, `DR-26`), just not living instruction or tradition.

## Case study: Juan Carlos

Juan Carlos (Palmer subnet) was founded on a pre-exile Spanish research station. Its own source material once framed its defining civic institution (a "tertulia") and its cuisine/music/arts as directly inherited from Spain — corrected in InnerTepeniaGDD to note any such institution would have to be independently developed by the exile community, with real-world echoes noted only as coincidence, never as cause. This repo's own Juan Carlos entry (`Worldspace/Locations/Juan_Carlos.md`) goes a step further and omits the unconfirmed cultural institution entirely rather than porting it over in reframed form, pending real independent development.
