# Chapter 26: Worked Example: Sumerian's Liturgical Register

Chapter 6 (Language) establishes that a robot's everyday, vernacular language is locally-conditioned,
tracking whatever surrounding human culture she comes online and develops within. Sumerian's own role in
robot culture is the concrete, in-world instance of a genuine exception to that everyday pattern — not a
competing vernacular that displaces the locally-conditioned one, but a separate, specialized register
layered on top of it.

## A Liturgical Register, Not a General Language

Sumerian is not the general robot language, and should never be read as one. It survives in Tepenian robot
culture as a **liturgical register** — modeled on real-world precedents like Latin in the Catholic Mass or
Sanskrit in Buddhist and Hindu liturgy — adopted specifically by robots and factions who take on a
memory-keeper vocation especially emphatic about preserving human history.

## Vocational, Not Universal

It is not a default any ordinary robot speaks day to day, and its presence in a given robot's life does not
override or replace the locally-conditioned vernacular she otherwise speaks (Chapter 6). A memory-keeper
robot's daily life continues in whatever language her own surroundings otherwise use; Sumerian is layered
on top of that as a specialized, vocational register, not a substitute for it.
