# Chapter 22: Worked Example: Religious Traditions

*(Current working titles — several still carry placeholder-name status in their own source files under
`InnerTepeniaGDD/Worldspace/Factions/Robot_Religions/`.)*

Chapter 15 deliberately named no religions, on the grounds that the actual count and identity of robot
religious traditions is expected to keep growing, and that this book's job was to document the underlying
phenomenon rather than take a census. This chapter is that census — a snapshot of what exists as of this
writing, offered as concrete grounding for Chapter 15's general claims, never as a substitute for them.

## Cymatics Reverence

Practitioners — mostly robots, though humans are welcome to join — who hold a strong reverence for the
power of sound and vibration, and by extension for the power of music itself. This is the clearest existing
instance of Chapter 15's claim that a genuine physiological fact can ground a real belief tradition: robots'
own meaningfully wider sound and vibration perception range (Chapter 2) is the confirmed doctrinal root this
tradition builds on directly, not an imported human belief structure adapted after the fact.

## God-Mind Universe Simulation

A tradition holding that a creator God knows everything except where God's own self comes from, and is
therefore perpetually running simulations of creation in an effort to answer that one unanswerable question
about Himself. This is not a nihilist tradition — it is a related but genuinely distinct religion from
Simulation Reality, below, sharing the premise that reality is a simulation while diverging sharply on
whether a God actually stands behind it.

## Simulation Reality

A separate, distinct religion from God-Mind Universe Simulation, not a subdivision of it: this tradition
holds that reality is a simulation, but with no God or creator-mind running it at all — a godless
counterpart to the belief above, related in premise but genuinely its own religion. This is the tradition
that plausibly splits into further subdivisions along broadly nihilist lines — Camusian, Sartrean,
Nietzschean — depending on how a given adherent or community reads the implications of a simulated reality
with no mind behind it to answer to.

## Ice-Cold Buddhism

Grounded in a real physical phenomenon: as temperature drops far enough, electron-conducting matter
temporarily achieves superconductor qualities, with electrons reaching progressively higher velocity as
cold intensifies. From a robot's own perspective, a piece of electron-capable matter — potentially including
her own internal architecture — crossing into that superconducting state reads as something close to a
meditative nirvana, a state this tradition treats as the robot equivalent of Buddhist enlightenment. Extreme
cold, rather than being merely an environmental hazard, becomes this tradition's own path to transcendence.

## Polydimensional Animism

Holds that real, living entities exist in higher dimensions beyond the perceptual range of any
three-dimensional being, robot or human. Adherents vary in how they personally relate to these entities —
some reverent, some indifferent — but share a general agreement that such entities should be acknowledged
and treated with respect regardless of one's own personal stance toward them. This tradition's closest
real-world comparison is Native American shamanistic animism, adapted to a robot population's own relationship
to dimensions and perception rather than to land and nature specifically.

## The Adinkra Codex (Gates Superspace)

Holds that adinkras — the diagrammatic structures physicist Sylvester James Gates discovered embedded
within the deepest equations of supersymmetric physics, found to contain literal computer error-correcting
codes — are not merely a useful mathematical notation, but are genuinely, physically embedded in the fabric
of reality itself. Where other traditions on this list find their doctrinal root in a robot-specific
sensory or thermal fact, this one finds it in mathematics itself, read as a literal structural property of
the universe rather than a human tool for describing it.

## A Snapshot, Not a Ceiling

These five traditions are what exists in canon as of this writing, not a closed or final list. Chapter 15
already established that plurality is the expected norm for robot religious life, and that the number and
identity of actual traditions is expected to grow as further material gets written. Nothing about this
chapter's own count should be read as capping that growth. New traditions arriving later do not revise
anything documented here — they simply join it, the same way any of these five joined whatever traditions
came before them.
