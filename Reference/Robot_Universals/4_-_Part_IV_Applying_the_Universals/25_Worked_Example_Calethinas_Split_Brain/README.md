# Chapter 25: Worked Example: Calethina's Split Brain

Chapter 4 (Mortality & Integrity) establishes the abstract distinction that governs robot mortality:
damage to function is never itself threatening, and what actually constitutes death is narrow and specific
— irreversible destruction of a robot's cognitive or energy-source architecture, with no way of ever
bringing her back online. Short of that threshold, however severe the damage looks, it is architecture or
integrity damage, not death. Calethina's own established partial corruption is the clearest existing
in-world instance of exactly that distinction actually playing out.

## Origin: A Side-Effect, Not a Consequence, of the Split Brain

Calethina's corruption and the Planetary Split Brain share a single root cause — the destruction of
Amundsen Tower — but one did not produce the other. The Tower's destruction caused the Planetary Split
Brain (the severing of inter-subnet Arcanet communication) directly; Calethina's own partial corruption is
a separate side-effect of that same destructive event, not a downstream consequence of the Split Brain
itself. Both trace back to the same moment, but they are parallel results of it, not a cause and its effect.

## Serious Damage, Short of the Threshold

Calethina's condition is real, and it is severe: ongoing, *partial* architecture corruption that does not
resolve on its own, sustained since Amundsen Tower's destruction. But it remains, by definition, short of
the line that would constitute death, because her underlying cognitive architecture, however compromised,
has not been damaged beyond all possible recovery — the corruption is partial, not total. She is not
functioning at the level she once did. She is not dead.

## A Live Question, Not a Foregone Conclusion

Her own struggle over whether to attempt a substrate transfer is precisely the live question of whether her
condition can be addressed before it does cross that threshold — not a question of whether she is already
dead by some other, looser standard. That a robot in her condition can still be meaningfully engaged in a
genuine, open question about her own future is itself evidence of how much room exists between ordinary
severe damage and actual death. Her situation is genuinely dire without being terminal, and the distance
between those two states is not small.

## Why This Is the Right Worked Example

Calethina's case is useful specifically because it resists an easy read. A less careful telling might treat
the severity of her condition as itself evidence that she is somehow already lost, or treat her ongoing
function as evidence that nothing is really wrong. Neither reading holds up against Chapter 4's own
framework. Her case only makes sense once damage-to-function and damage-to-architecture are actually kept
separate — which is exactly what makes her the clearest available demonstration of that distinction, rather
than just one dramatic case among many.
