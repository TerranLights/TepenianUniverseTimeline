# Chapter 21: Worked Example: Consumption Culture

Chapter 16 established the structural basis of robot consumption and vice culture without naming any of its
actual instances. This chapter is where those instances live — the concrete substances and practices that
already exist in canon, and the clearest, most fully worked demonstration of the differentiation engine
(Chapter 18) operating on an entire category at once, rather than a single isolated case.

## Siligel

Siligel is the established robot equivalent of food: a substance consumed to maintain and repair internal
systems. Its exact composition and the full range of what it does internally remain open for further
development, but its basic role — robot food, in the fullest sense — is confirmed canon.

## Coolant

Robots manage internal heat through coolant systems, and coolant itself functions as the robot equivalent
of drinking: a liquid intake with genuine physiological purpose, thermal regulation, rather than a
metaphorical stand-in for human beverages. Like human drinks, coolant exists in a real range of
formulations, qualities, and varieties. A robot drinking coolant after hard work occupies the same
functional and cultural space a human drinking water after exertion does.

## Robot Coffee

Robot coffee is a specialty coolant formulation that produces particular internal effects beyond basic
thermal regulation. Its exact composition remains open, but it sits within the established coolant
category — consumed for the experience it produces, not purely for temperature management. Its existence as
its own distinct product confirms something already established in Chapter 16: robots consume things for
pleasure and experience, not maintenance alone.

## Smoking (via Internal Thermal/Energy Systems)

Robots have internal atmospheric management systems — not for respiration, but for thermal regulation and
sensory input processing. These systems move air or vapor-equivalent substances through internal chambers
as part of managing heat and environmental awareness. Drawing vapor through these systems creates real,
detectable internal states, giving robot smoking a genuine physiological mechanism rather than a purely
social gesture borrowed from human custom.

## Glitch-Coolant

Glitch-coolant is a specialty coolant formulation, mixed with a scientific concoction of electrolytic
compounds, that produces a deliberate "glitching" effect analogous to human drunkenness — the robot
equivalent of alcohol, consumed recreationally after a work shift rather than for thermal regulation. This
is a genuine Tepenia-wide robot cultural institution, not a single-city drink, and it is the clearest
already-existing proof that this whole category varies by population exactly the way Chapter 18 predicts.

**Flagged for future development, not urgent:** "glitch-coolant" is a working, descriptive placeholder, the
same way "firewater" would be if it were the only term ever used for alcohol in a human setting. The
substance eventually needs its own proper, in-world name, the way "alcohol" is a real term rather than a
placeholder for the thing itself. This does not block anything — scenes, settings, micro-cultures, and
personal stories can all be built around glitch-coolant perfectly well without its final name being settled
first. It is simply a known open item, not a blocker.

Bohemian and cosmopolitan cities support a wide *variety* of glitch-coolant types — different formulations,
different characters — the robot equivalent of a sophisticated human drinking culture offering beers, ales,
lagers, wines, whiskeys, and vodkas side by side, prizing variety and refinement over raw intensity.
Working-class robot settings carry a much more limited selection, but what is available produces a
noticeably *stronger* glitching effect than the bohemian cities' milder, more varied options — fewer
choices, more potency, a direct and unpretentious drinking culture rather than a refined one.

## Condition-Derived Variation Across the Category

Glitch-coolant's own variety-versus-potency split is not an isolated curiosity. It is the clearest working
proof-of-concept, already established in canon before this book existed, that the entire consumption and
vice category is exactly the kind of substantive cultural material the differentiation engine is built to
handle: condition (a city's own class character and economic life) producing genuine variation (which
formulations exist, and what they emphasize), on top of a universal capacity for consumption and vice that
never itself varies. The same logic that shapes glitch-coolant's own regional character plausibly extends
to siligel, coolant, robot coffee, and smoking as well, even where the specific details of that variation
remain open for future development.
